1. Agreement to these terms

These terms are the agreement between RXNAIJA HEALTH LTD (CAC 8345514) and the pharmacy that uses RxNaija. By signing in at app.rxnaija.com, or by letting us operate a WhatsApp assistant for your pharmacy, you accept them.

If you are accepting on behalf of a pharmacy, you are confirming that you are authorised to bind it. Where that is true, "you" in this document means the pharmacy, and everyone you give an account to is covered by it.

They are written the same way as our privacy policy — to be read, rather than to be survived.

2. What RxNaija is, and what it is not

RxNaija assists your own staff with ordering and enquiries on WhatsApp. It answers questions about your catalogue, takes orders against your stock, and keeps the record of what was said.

It does not practise pharmacy, and it does not give clinical advice. A question about a dose, a child, pregnancy, an interaction, or a symptom is handed to a registered pharmacist rather than answered. That is not a setting you can turn off, and it is not a disclaimer we are asking you to accept in place of the behaviour — it is how the software is built.

  • Clinical judgement remains yours. Every clinical decision taken in your pharmacy is taken by your registered staff, on their own responsibility and under their own licence.
  • We do not supply, dispense or handle medicines, and we are not a party to the sale between you and your customer.
  • We are not a medical device and we do not diagnose.

3. Who these terms bind

They bind the pharmacy and the people it gives accounts to. They do not bind your customers: someone messaging your pharmacy on WhatsApp is in a relationship with you, not with us, and section 2 of our privacy policy explains the same split for their data.

You confirm that your pharmacy is lawfully operating and, where the law requires it, registered and supervised by a pharmacist. We may ask for evidence of that, and may decline or withdraw service if it is not forthcoming.

4. Accounts and access

  • An account belongs to a person, not to a role. Sharing one makes the audit trail — who confirmed an order, who answered a clinical question — untrue, and that record is the thing that protects your staff.
  • You are responsible for keeping sign-in details secure, and for what is done under your accounts.
  • Tell us promptly if you believe an account has been compromised.
  • Each pharmacy's data is separated at the database level and that separation is enforced by the server. Attempting to reach another pharmacy's data is a breach of these terms and, in all likelihood, of the law.

  • harass, threaten or abuse our staff.

6. Messaging people on WhatsApp

This section matters more than the rest of them put together, because getting it wrong costs your customers their peace and can cost your pharmacy its WhatsApp number.

  • The assistant replies; it does not solicit. It answers people who wrote to your pharmacy first. You must not use it to contact people who have not.
  • Opt-outs are absolute. When someone asks to stop being messaged, they stop being messaged. You must not work around that, and neither will we.
  • Consent is per purpose. Someone who wanted order updates has not agreed to marketing. The software keeps those apart and you must not defeat it.
  • The provider's rules bind you too. WhatsApp may restrict or disconnect a number that behaves like spam, and we cannot restore access it has withdrawn.

We may pause outbound messaging for a pharmacy if we reasonably believe it is being used against these rules. We would rather interrupt your service than help you lose your number.

We work to keep the service running, but we do not promise it will be uninterrupted. Maintenance, provider outages, network failures and WhatsApp disconnections all happen, and some are outside our control.
